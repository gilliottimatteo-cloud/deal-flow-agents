## Company summary

- **What it does:** turns a shop owner's Android phone into a card terminal (tap-to-phone), plus free QR payments by bank transfer, next-business-day payouts and a digital sales ledger. No hardware (deck slide 3).
- **Problem:** neighborhood shops (tienditas) run mostly on cash, and a conventional terminal means rent, a contract and days of waiting for the money (deck slide 2, company claim).
- **Business model:** three revenue lines on one app. Card payments (merchant pays 3.2% + IVA, Monedari nets 1.17% of card volume), Monedari Plus (USD 6.50 a month, 2,600 subscribers) and Adelanto cash advances (fixed fee per advance, 640 advances since February 2026). QR is free (deck slides 4, 7).
- **Traction headline:** net revenue 3.2x year over year (USD 31K to USD 100K a month), 14,200 active merchants, USD 9.8M monthly volume (deck slide 8).
- **Distribution:** own field force plus two regional wholesalers, which brought 46% of 2026 new merchants (deck slide 10).

## First-call highlights

Call of 2026-09-22 with the CEO and CTO (Tomas G. and Lucia M. for the fund).

- **Cash is tight.** About USD 1.05M in the bank, net burn about USD 150K a month, runway about seven months. The USD 1.7M committed is already wired and already inside that cash figure. April 2026 was "very tight" before the lead's money arrived.
- **One acquiring bank.** All card processing runs through a single bank under an aggregator agreement that renews in June 2027. A second acquirer is "advanced" but not signed.
- **Adelanto is funded from the balance sheet.** Losses are "around 7% of what we lent". Nobody on the team has a credit or risk background, and the risk rules are written by the CTO and a data engineer.
- **Former co-founder holds 4%.** Andres P. left in August 2024. The CEO believes the shares are vested. The separation agreement is promised for the data room. There is no CFO, only a part-time finance contractor.
- **Retention gain is confounded.** Six-month retention rose from 62% (2025 cohorts) to 71% (2026 cohorts), but the wholesaler channel also grew. Cohorts by channel are promised.

## Competitive context

**Deck names four competitor groups.** Clip, Mercado Pago and bank terminals are named, plus Bold in Colombia (deck slide 11). The deck table compares only on hardware. No pricing, feature or share comparison is given. The deck says the others "sell or rent a card terminal and are much larger", which is the company's claim and is not verified. Nothing here is researched, and whether the hardware-free edge holds is a question for the competition stage.

**Colombia is thinner than the deck reads.** Slides 6 and 14 say Colombia is "live" with 1,300 merchants. The call clarified that Colombia is QR only, and QR is free to the merchant (deck slide 7). So about 9% of active merchants likely generate almost no take revenue (INFERRED), while Colombia gets 20% of the use of funds. Colombia unit economics were not discussed.

## Market context

**The deck's market claim is unverified.** The deck claims 5 million small shops in Latin America, a "USD 12B market" and 1.2 million neighborhood shops in Mexico (deck slide 6). It does not define the USD 12B (volume, revenue pool or something else) and cites no source. By the deck's own numbers, 12,900 Mexican merchants is about 1.1% of the 1.2 million shops (INFERRED). Sizing is for the market stage.

## Gut feeling

The team fit is strong: a founder who sold to these shops for seven years and a CTO who built the payment core, with growth, margin and payback all at or near the fund's bars. The wholesaler channel is the smartest part of the story, at USD 24 CAC against USD 58 for field agents. What concerns me is fragility: seven months of cash, one acquirer with a June 2027 renewal, and a balance-sheet lending product run without credit expertise. The 71% retention gain also cannot yet be separated from channel mix. None of this is disqualifying at first pass, but each needs a hard look.

## Recommendation

**ADVANCE.** Proceed to the market and competition analysis. The commercial case clears every hard filter and most of the ideal-profile signals. The first-pass risks are structural and answerable in diligence. Priority questions to carry forward:

- **Acquirer dependence:** key terms of the aggregator agreement, termination and repricing rights, and the real status of the second acquirer.
- **Runway and round mechanics:** only about USD 1.3M of new money remains in this round. Financial model, burn plan and the cap and terms of every SAFE already signed are PENDING.
- **Adelanto:** total amount lent, tenor, fee against the 7% loss rate, and the regulatory standing of lending and of the aggregator structure. All PENDING.
- **Wholesaler concentration:** terms of the 2-year exclusive partnership (who is bound is not clear from the deck) and cohorts split by channel.
- **Cap table:** founder stakes, option pool, vesting of the 4% held by Andres P., and the number of investors.
- **Colombia:** card acceptance plan and unit economics.
