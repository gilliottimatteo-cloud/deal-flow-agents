## Verdict

**RED ocean, two narrow REAL moats.** Monedari's headline edge, card acceptance on the shop's own phone with no hardware, is no longer unique. Clip launched Tap to Pay for Android in January 2026 and Mercado Pago launched Point Tap in March 2026, both six months or more before the September 2026 deck that still shows them as terminal-only. Free QR, Monedari's second hook, is being turned into a public, zero-fee utility by Banxico (CoDi) in Mexico and by Bre-B in Colombia. What Monedari really holds is a live wholesaler channel and a founding team that knows the tiendita. Both are real but narrow, and neither is yet verified beyond the company's own figures.

### Moat verdict

**Two of seven moats are REAL, both narrow.** Distribution is REAL: two live wholesaler partnerships brought 46% of 2026 new merchants (deck slide 10, company claim, contract not seen). Founder and sector is REAL: a CEO with seven years selling to tienditas and a CTO from a bank's acquiring unit (deck slide 13, references PENDING). Product and tech, data, investor and regulatory are CLAIMED or absent. Physical infrastructure does not apply. To turn claimed moats real, Monedari would need: the second acquirer signed and its own risk engine live with a proven loss rate, a lead investor who is named and opening doors, and evidence that its shop data (cash ledger plus card flows) predicts credit risk better than the larger players' data.

### Ocean verdict

**RED.** The two best-funded payment players in Mexico shipped Monedari's core feature in 2026, banks and the central bank are piling in, and Monedari's real moats (a regional wholesaler channel and founder know-how) are not enough to hold off rivals with 100x its capital.

### Top three open competitive questions

1. Since Clip Tap (January 2026) and Point Tap (March 2026) launched, have Monedari's merchant wins, churn and pricing held? Why does the deck still call them terminal-only? (OPEN)
2. How strong is the wholesaler channel: who is bound by the 2-year exclusivity, on what terms, and do channel cohorts retain better? (OPEN)
3. What happens to card volume and the 1.17% take when CoDi offers zero-fee QR to every small merchant from November 2026, and why would a Colombian shop use Monedari's QR over its own bank's Bre-B QR? (OPEN)

**The deck's competition slide is out of date.** Slide 11 compares Monedari only on hardware and says Clip and Mercado Pago sell a card terminal. Both had launched a no-hardware, phone-as-terminal product before the deck's September 2026 date: Clip on 28 January 2026 and Mercado Pago from 12 March 2026. Apple's Tap to Pay on iPhone arrived in Mexico on 26 March 2026 with Clip and Mercado Pago among the launch platforms. Either management missed the launches or left them out. Both readings matter for the team assessment, and the question goes to the next call.

**Pricing is a thin edge, payout is a gap.** Monedari charges 3.2% + IVA per card payment (deck slide 7). Mercado Pago's Point Tap list price is 3.5% + IVA with no monthly fee, and the press reported a 2.99% launch promotion for the first three months. Monedari's 0.3-point discount on list price is real but easy to match. On payout speed Monedari is behind: next business day, against Mercado Pago's instant credit to its own account. Clip's Tap to Pay rate was not found in this research and is PENDING.

**Average tickets are unknown across the field.** Neither the deck nor public sources give average tickets. The tap limits (MXN 1,000 for Clip, MXN 3,000 for Mercado Pago) are far above typical tiendita baskets, so they do not limit competition in this segment (INFERRED). Monedari's average ticket is PENDING: card volume is given, transaction count is not.

**Credit is crowded too.** Adelanto, 640 advances since February 2026 and funded from the balance sheet, competes with Clip's loans, Bold's credit, and three tiendita-specific lenders. Propaga pays the supplier directly, gives the shop 15 days, and works through CPG partners such as Nestle and Modelo. Tienda Pago and YoFio have raised USD 12M and USD 10M. Monedari's only edge here is that it sees the shop's daily card sales and can deduct repayment from them. That is useful, but Clip, Mercado Pago and Bold can do the same with their own sales data.

**Physical infrastructure is not applicable.** Monedari is hardware-free by design, so it has no deployed assets that raise a capital barrier. That is a cost advantage over terminal fleets, not a moat, and the 2026 tap-to-phone launches have closed even that gap.

**Two REAL moats, both narrow and both company-reported.** Distribution through wholesalers is the one asset that could become a lasting wedge: a supplier's route rep already has the shop owner's trust and visits weekly. Founder sector knowledge explains why the channel exists. Neither stops a well-funded rival that signs its own wholesaler or CPG partner.

## Value added

**The bundle is the claim, the channel is the wedge.** Monedari's pitch combines acceptance, a cash-and-card ledger, supplier ordering (Plus), and cash advances repaid from card sales, sold by the people who already restock the shop. Taken one by one, every piece is matched by someone. Acceptance is matched by Clip and Mercado Pago, ordering by Juntos+, and credit by Clip, Propaga and Tienda Pago. Only the delivery through wholesaler route reps is distinctive, and only as far as the exclusivity terms and channel cohorts prove it.

**Copyable features, possibly durable channel.** The no-hardware feature is already copied. Free QR is being given away by the state. The ledger and Plus inventory tools are a few months of product work for a funded team (INFERRED). The wholesaler channel is harder to copy, because partners must be signed one at a time and exclusivity blocks the largest one for two years. But the biggest supplier networks (Coca-Cola FEMSA) run their own platforms and could pick Clip or Mercado Pago as a payments partner instead. The wedge is real only if wholesaler-sourced merchants retain and monetize better. The call said retention rose from 62% to 71%, but that gain is confounded with channel mix, and cohorts split by channel are PENDING.

| Cash advances repaid from card sales | Already done | Clip loans, Bold credit; Mercado Pago credit offer not verified in this research |

**The response window is short.** Every product element is already copied or copyable within six months. Only the wholesaler channel buys time, and that time is measured partner by partner. The moat is thin.

## Incumbents' structural barriers

**Weak barriers, recorded as a risk.** Nothing in the market's structure stops Clip or Mercado Pago from serving tienditas. Both already reach small merchants at scale, and Mercado Pago reports more than 1 million active POS terminals in Mexico. Three partial frictions exist:

- **Cost to serve low-digital shops.** Onboarding a shop owner who has never taken a digital payment (61% of Monedari's base, deck slide 12, company claim) may need an in-person visit. Self-serve digital funnels miss them, and route reps reach them cheaply (INFERRED). This is a friction for incumbents, not a wall.
- **Exclusivity with the largest wholesaler.** Two years locks one regional route against rivals, if the exclusivity binds the wholesaler and not only Monedari. That is PENDING.
- **CPG platforms say they are not fintechs.** Coca-Cola FEMSA states it does not see itself as a fintech, which makes it a potential partner rather than a direct rival. The same platform is building payment acceptance and microcredit, though, so this cuts both ways.

**The acquirer is also a latent rival.** Monedari processes through a single bank and describes itself as that bank's "biggest tap-to-phone client" (call). That bank therefore sees Monedari's volumes and could serve tienditas directly, as BBVA and BanBajio already do through their own apps. Renewal is June 2027, about two months after runway ends (pre-screen).

## Ocean verdict

**RED.** Density is high and credible. Clip (USD 500M raised in June 2026), Mercado Pago (1M+ POS in Mexico), at least three banks and processors, the central bank's own zero-fee rails, and in Colombia Bold and Bre-B all compete for the same shop and the same transaction. Monedari's relative moat strength is low. Its two REAL moats, a regional wholesaler channel and founder know-how, are narrow, not yet verified, and do not cover the core product. The feature the deck builds its competitive case on was copied by the two leaders months before the deck was written. The space is crowded and the company does not have a clear moat to hold rivals off.

**What would move it to YELLOW.** First, evidence that wholesaler-sourced merchants retain and monetize clearly better. Second, an exclusivity that binds the wholesalers and covers a meaningful share of routes. Third, a second acquirer signed. Fourth, stable merchant wins and churn through Clip's and Mercado Pago's 2026 launches. Together these would show a defended niche inside a crowded market.

## Sector insights

**Tap-to-phone went mainstream in Mexico in 2026.** BanBajio launched BBclik with Visa for tienditas and street vendors (16 January 2026). Clip launched Tap to Pay for Android (28 January 2026), Mercado Pago launched Point Tap (from 12 March 2026), Apple's Tap to Pay on iPhone launched with Adyen, Clip, Mercado Pago and Visa (26 March 2026), and Broxel added it in June 2026. *Implication:* "no terminal needed" is now table stakes. Monedari must win on channel, payout, price or bundle, and its device coverage (Android only per deck slide 3) is a gap against iPhone-enabled rivals.

**Clip raised USD 500M and is targeting cash users directly.** On 9 June 2026 Clip announced USD 500M at a valuation above USD 2.5B, pending regulatory approval. It launched a wallet built with Ant International, Mastercard and TelevisaUnivision and plans to grow cash deposit points from 28,000 to 100,000. *Implication:* the market leader is spending against the same cash-heavy segment, with capital Monedari cannot match. Acquisition by Clip or a peer is a plausible exit, and Clip is also a pricing threat.

**Mexico is making QR and transfers free for small merchants.** Banxico opened a consultation on simpler SPEI and CoDi use (19 March 2026). It published rules in June 2026 that require banks to surface CoDi and DiMo and that create a "Nivel 2 Bis" account for small merchants paid mostly digitally. A Ley de Economia Digital, sent to Congress on 9 September 2026, proposes zero fees on SPEI and CoDi for small merchants, and the CoDi rollout in shops is expected from the first half of November 2026. *Implication:* Monedari's free QR stops being a hook. If shops push customers from cards to free transfers, Monedari's card take (1.17%) is at risk. The wider push to digitize shops is a tailwind for onboarding.

**Bre-B makes Colombia's QR-only offer a commodity.** Colombia's instant-payment system launched on 6 October 2025 and passed 34M registered users and 670M transactions in six months (Banco de la Republica, 13 April 2026). Banks such as Bancolombia and BBVA (via Credibanco) now offer interoperable merchant QR. *Implication:* Monedari's 1,300 Colombian merchants use a QR-only product that every bank now offers. Colombia, which takes 20% of the use of funds, needs card acceptance through a local acquirer to have any edge, and there it meets Bold.

**Suppliers are building their own tendero platforms.** Coca-Cola FEMSA's Juntos+ reported more than 1.4 million active tenderos in Latin America (November 2025), 40.4 million digital orders in 2024, and teams building payment acceptance and microcredit. Propaga already reaches shops through Nestle and Modelo. *Implication:* the distribution channel Monedari depends on is contested. The largest supplier networks may build or pick their own payments partner, which limits how far Monedari's wholesaler model can scale. The same suppliers are also the most natural partners or acquirers.
