# Monedari: seed deck, September 2026

## Slide 1: Monedari

- Every corner shop can take a card. No terminal needed.
- Payments, next-day money and growth tools for Latin America's neighborhood shops.
- Seed round: USD 3M
- Mexico City | September 2026 | Confidential

## Slide 2: The problem

- The tiendita is the backbone of the Latin American neighborhood: groceries, snacks, phone top-ups, on credit if you know the owner.
- Most of them still run on cash.
- Customers increasingly pay by card or transfer. When the shop can't accept it, the sale walks to the convenience chain down the street.
- A card terminal means a monthly rent or an upfront cost, a contract, and waiting days for the money.
- Banks don't visit tienditas. Nobody has built for them.

## Slide 3: Our solution

- Monedari turns the shop owner's Android phone into a payment terminal.
- **Tap-to-phone:** customers tap their card or phone on the merchant's phone.
- **QR by bank transfer:** free for the merchant.
- **Next business day:** money lands in the shop's bank account.
- **Digital ledger:** every sale recorded automatically, cash sales too.
- No hardware, live in 10 minutes.

## Slide 4: The product

- **Monedari app (free):** card and QR acceptance, daily sales ledger, payout tracking.
- **Monedari Plus (USD 6.50 a month):** inventory, supplier orders, weekly sales reports. 2,600 subscribers and growing.
- **Adelanto (NEW, since February 2026):** working capital for shops, repaid automatically as a share of daily card sales. No paperwork, no branch visit. 640 advances to date.
- Built on our own payment core, connected directly to our acquiring bank partner.

## Slide 5: How a shop joins

1. A Monedari agent or a wholesaler's route rep visits the shop.
2. The owner downloads the app, takes an ID photo and adds a bank account.
3. First sale the same visit.
- Customer support by WhatsApp, in Spanish, seven days a week.

## Slide 6: A huge, underserved market

- 5 million small shops in Latin America. A USD 12B market.
- 1.2 million neighborhood shops in Mexico alone.
- Digital payments in Mexico are growing fast, but the corner shop has been left behind.
- Colombia: a second large tiendita market, where we are already live.

## Slide 7: Business model

- **Card payments:** merchant pays 3.2% + IVA per card payment. Our net take after interchange and acquirer costs: 1.17% of card volume.
- **QR payments:** free. They bring shops in and keep them active.
- **Monedari Plus:** about USD 6.50 a month per subscriber.
- **Adelanto:** a fixed fee per advance.
- Three revenue lines from the same merchant, on the same app.

## Slide 8: Traction

|---|---|---|
| Active merchants | 5,300 | 14,200 |
| Monthly payment volume | USD 3.7M | USD 9.8M |
| Monthly net revenue | USD 31K | USD 100K |

- 3.2x net revenue year over year. USD 1.2M annualized.
- 9% average monthly growth over the last 6 months.
- Payment volume: 62% card, 38% QR.
- Active merchant = at least one transaction in the last 30 days.

## Slide 9: Unit economics

- Gross margin: 54%, up from 48% a year ago.
- Customer acquisition cost: USD 41 per activated merchant (blended).
- Gross profit per active merchant: about USD 3.80 a month.
- Payback: about 11 months.
- Six-month retention: 71% for 2026 cohorts, up from 62% for 2025 cohorts.

## Slide 10: Distribution that scales

- Our own field force in Mexico City, Estado de Mexico, Puebla and Jalisco.
- Partnerships with two regional consumer-goods wholesalers: their route reps already visit thousands of shops every week, and now they sign them up for Monedari.
- 46% of new merchants in 2026 came through wholesalers.
- Wholesaler acquisition cost: USD 24 per merchant, versus USD 58 for our field agents.
- 2-year exclusive partnership with our largest wholesaler partner.

## Slide 11: Competition

| | Monedari | Clip | Mercado Pago | Bank terminals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hardware | None, the shop's own phone | Card terminal | Card terminal | Rented terminal |

- In Colombia: Bold.
- What we add on top of acceptance: next-day money, a ledger with inventory and supplier orders, working capital, and the wholesaler routes.
- The others sell or rent a card terminal and are much larger. We win on the shop floor: no hardware, distribution through the wholesalers who already visit the shop, and tools made for how a tiendita works.

## Slide 12: Impact

- 61% of our merchants had never accepted a digital payment before Monedari.
- 58% of our shops are run by women.
- Merchants report sales up 11% after six months on Monedari.
- Every new shop on Monedari is a shop entering the formal financial system.
