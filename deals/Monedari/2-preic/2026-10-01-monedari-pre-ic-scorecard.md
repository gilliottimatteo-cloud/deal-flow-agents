## Recommendation

**ADVANCE, 65 out of 100, on a thin margin.** The total clears the Pre-IC threshold of 60 by five points, and the S6 impact gate holds at 3. But the total rests on a strong team and strong reported traction, sitting on top of a weak market score. Read as a pattern, Monedari is a well-run company in a revenue pool that, as sized today, cannot carry a 10x return. Diligence should therefore aim first at the one question that decides the case: can revenue per shop multiply through Adelanto and Monedari Plus, or can the target widen beyond grocery shops in two countries? Every score below rests on company claims from the deck and one call. None has been checked against a data room.

**Top three reasons to advance:**

1. **The founders fit the customer.** The CEO sold to neighborhood shops for seven years and the CTO built the payment core after working in a bank's acquiring unit. This is the seller-plus-builder pair the fund's ideal profile asks for (deck slide 13, call 2026-09-22).
2. **Reported traction clears the fund's bars.** Net revenue grew 3.2x year over year, 9% a month on average over six months, at a 54% gross margin (up from 48%) and about 11 months of payback (deck slides 8, 9). The deck's figures are internally consistent (pre-screen cross-check).
3. **The wholesaler channel is a real, cheap wedge.** Two wholesalers brought 46% of 2026 new merchants at USD 24 each, against USD 58 for field agents (deck slide 10). The competition analysis rates it the strongest REAL moat.

**Top three risks:**

1. **The market as sized is too small for the return.** A 10x return needs USD 50M of revenue at exit, which is 64% of the USD 78.4M SAM at today's revenue per shop (market analysis, reality test).
2. **The core product is no longer different.** Clip (January 2026) and Mercado Pago (March 2026) shipped tap-to-phone before the deck was written, and Mexico's central bank is making QR free for small merchants from November 2026 (competition analysis).
3. **The company is fragile on cash and on one bank.** Runway is about seven months, to around April 2027, two months before the single acquiring bank's agreement comes up for renewal in June 2027. The balance-sheet lending product is run without credit expertise (call 2026-09-22).

## Dimension scores

### S1 Team

- **Score:** 4/5
- **Rating:** STRONG
- **Key finding:** Clear founder-market fit, with a seller and a builder who know this customer. Two gaps are open, finance and credit risk, and only the first has a plan.
- **Evidence:** Deck slide 13 (CEO, CTO, COO backgrounds; 38 people); call 2026-09-22 (origin story, no CFO, no credit or risk background, former co-founder Andres P. left August 2024 holding 4%); competition analysis (deck slide 11 out of date).
- **Gap:** Founder references, the cap table, the separation agreement and vesting of Andres P.'s 4%, the finance-lead hiring plan, and management's answer on why slide 11 omits the 2026 tap-to-phone launches.

**The founder fit is genuine and specific.** Mariana R. spent seven years as a regional sales manager at a consumer-goods distributor selling to tienditas, and the idea came from shop owners asking her route reps for "the card thing" (call 2026-09-22). The wholesaler channel, the company's best asset, grows directly out of that background. Diego T. built the payment core himself after working in a bank's acquiring unit. Sofia L. (COO) runs the field force and the wholesaler channel (deck slide 13).

**Two important roles are open.** There is no CFO, only a part-time finance contractor, and a finance lead is "on the hiring plan for after the round" (call). That is a plan, if a thin one, for a company with seven months of cash. The more serious gap is credit: Adelanto lends from the balance sheet, and its risk rules are written by the CTO and a data engineer with no credit background (call). The use of funds includes a risk engine but no credit hire.

**Two questions could move this score to 3.** First, deck slide 11 still shows Clip and Mercado Pago as terminal-only, months after both launched tap-to-phone (competition analysis). Either management missed it or left it out, and both readings matter. Second, the cap table is unseen. Andres P.'s 4% is below the fund's 10% founder line, but its vesting is unconfirmed, and the founders' own stakes and full-time status are INFERRED (pre-screen, Founders filter).

### S2 Market

- **Score:** 2/5
- **Rating:** WEAK
- **Key finding:** The sizing is careful and two methods agree, but the reachable revenue pool is about USD 78M a year, and only USD 38M of it can earn revenue today. The return needs USD 50M of revenue at exit.
- **Evidence:** Market analysis 2026-09-29 (one-page summary, SOM band, bottom-up cross-check, reality test); deck slides 6 and 14.
- **Gap:** Evidence that revenue per shop can rise well above USD 93 a year (revenue split by line, Adelanto economics); the definition behind the deck's 1.2 million Mexican shops; the share of current merchants that are not grocery shops; Colombian acquirer timing.

**The method is strong, so the score reflects size, not quality.** The market analysis builds TAM (USD 102.6M) and SAM (USD 78.4M) from census and trade-association shop counts times Monedari's realized USD 93 of net revenue per shop per year. A bottom-up build reaches USD 6.06M by 2031, inside the bull case of USD 6.27M, so the two methods agree at the top of the band. Tailwinds are sourced: contactless rose from 2% to 30% of in-person card payments in Mexico in about two years, and card payments grew 14% in 2025.

**The pool is small against the return.** USD 50M is 64% of today's SAM, and still 33% at the USD 179 per shop the deck's 2029 plan implies (market analysis, reality test). The deck's own 2029 plan of USD 21M is well short of USD 50M. Half the SAM, the USD 40.1M in Colombia, depends on a local card acquirer that is not signed. Pulling the other way, tiendita sales are flat to falling in both countries, and free instant transfers cap the price of acceptance. Growth of 10% to 14% a year is INFERRED, not sourced for this pool.

**The deck overstates the market.** It claims 1.2 million neighborhood shops in Mexico against the census count of about 653,000 (INFERRED from census growth data), and a "USD 12B market" with no source or definition (deck slide 6, market analysis). The gap could be real upside if the deck counts shops the census misses, which is why it stays an open question rather than a red flag.

**What would move it to 3 or 4.** Proof that Adelanto and Monedari Plus lift revenue per shop toward USD 179 and beyond, or a credible path to a wider target (other micro-merchants, more countries).

### S3 Product and defensibility

- **Score:** 3/5
- **Rating:** MODERATE
- **Key finding:** Shops use and keep the product, but its headline feature is now matched by the two market leaders. The defensible part is the wholesaler channel and founder know-how, both real and both narrow.
- **Evidence:** Competition analysis 2026-09-29 (RED ocean verdict, moat table, response timeline); deck slides 3, 4, 9, 10, 11; call 2026-09-22 (single acquirer, retention discussion).
- **Gap:** The wholesaler contracts and who the 2-year exclusivity binds; cohorts split by channel; gross adds and churn from January to September 2026, across the Clip and Mercado Pago launches; card versus QR mix by month.

**Customers show need.** 14,200 active merchants, 2,600 paying Monedari Plus subscribers (about 18% attach, INFERRED), and six-month retention rising from 62% to 71% suggest shops rely on the product (deck slides 4, 8, 9). 61% of merchants had never accepted a digital payment before (deck slide 12, company claim).

**The product edge has closed.** Clip launched Tap to Pay for Android in January 2026 and Mercado Pago launched Point Tap in March 2026. BanBajio, BBVA and Broxel offer the same. Monedari's price is 0.3 points below Mercado Pago's list price, its payout is slower (next business day against instant), and it runs on Android only. Free QR is being made a zero-fee public utility by CoDi in Mexico and Bre-B in Colombia (competition analysis). Every product element is copied or copyable within six months.

**The wholesaler channel holds the score at 3.** It is a REAL moat in the competition analysis, with early proof in the deck's own numbers: 46% of new merchants and less than half the field-agent cost (deck slide 10). It is also narrow (two regional wholesalers against Coca-Cola FEMSA's own platform reaching 1.4 million shopkeepers) and company-reported, with the contract unseen. If channel cohorts show no retention edge or the exclusivity binds only Monedari, this score drops to 2.

**The acquiring bank is a dependency and a possible rival.** All card processing runs through one bank under an aggregator agreement renewing June 2027. That bank sees all of Monedari's volume and could serve tienditas directly, as other banks already do (call, competition analysis).

### S4 Traction and unit economics

- **Score:** 4/5
- **Rating:** STRONG
- **Key finding:** Growth, margin and payback all clear the fund's bars, and margin and retention are moving the right way. Burn efficiency misses the bar, and every number is a company claim.
- **Evidence:** Deck slides 8, 9, 14; call 2026-09-22 (cash, burn, Adelanto losses, retention by cohort); pre-screen (consistency check, burn multiple).
- **Gap:** The financial model and monthly KPIs; cohorts by channel; the Adelanto portfolio file (amount lent, tenor, fee, losses); Colombia unit economics; the split of the residual USD 12K a month of revenue.

| Signal | Fund bar | Monedari (company claim) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | 8% a month or 2.5x a year | 9% a month (6-month average), 3.2x a year | Meets |
| Gross margin | Above 50% | 54% (48% a year earlier) | Meets, improving |
| CAC payback | 12 months or less | About 11 months (USD 41 CAC, USD 3.80 gross profit per merchant per month) | Just meets |
| Net burn vs net new annual revenue | Below 1.5x | About 2.2x (USD 1.8M a year vs USD 0.83M), INFERRED | Misses |
| Revenue history | 12+ months | Data from September 2025 | Meets |

**The growth story holds together.** Monthly net revenue went from USD 31K to USD 100K and active merchants from 5,300 to 14,200 between September 2025 and September 2026 (deck slide 8). The pre-screen checked the deck's arithmetic: revenue per merchant, gross profit per merchant and payback all reconcile.

**Three things keep it from a 5.** First, burn is inefficient at about 2.2x net new annual revenue, against the fund's 1.5x bar (pre-screen, INFERRED). Second, the retention gain from 62% to 71% is confounded with the growing wholesaler channel, which the CEO partly conceded (call). Only the cohorts that joined by March 2026 can have six full months of history by September, so the 71% likely rests on a small, early slice of 2026 (INFERRED). Third, Adelanto losses are "around 7% of what we lent" with no file yet, funded from scarce equity (call).

**Colombia adds merchants but little revenue.** Colombia's 1,300 merchants are QR only, and QR is free (call, deck slide 7), so about 9% of active merchants likely earn almost nothing (INFERRED).

**Projections run ahead of the market.** The plan goes from USD 0.95M net revenue in 2026 to USD 21M in 2029, with 150,000 merchants (deck slide 14). That is more than twice the bull share of SAM in the market analysis. It does not lower the traction score, but it is not earned by history yet.

### S5 Deal and return

- **Score:** 3/5
- **Rating:** MODERATE
- **Key finding:** The ticket fits and the price is fair, but the return check lands at 42x, in the stretch band, on a placeholder multiple, and the market analysis says the market cannot easily support it. The lead is unnamed and most of the committed money is already spent into the runway.
- **Evidence:** Deck slide 15 (round, cap, use of funds); call 2026-09-22 (USD 500K ask, rolling close, cash and burn); pre-screen (valuation multiple, runway); market analysis (reality test); fund-thesis return check.
- **Gap:** The lead fund's name and terms; the cap and terms of every SAFE already signed; a researched exit multiple (comparables, strategic buyers); the burn plan after the round.

**The round fits the fund's terms.** USD 500K is the fund's standard first ticket. The round is USD 3M, above the USD 2M the fund can lead, so Example Ventures would co-invest behind the existing lead. 57% is committed and already wired (deck slide 15, call).

**The price is fair, the return is a stretch.** The USD 12M cap is 10.0x annualized net revenue, on a net basis that does not flatter (pre-screen, comparison not researched). The return check below lands at 42x required growth. A stretch is defensible only with conviction on team and market, and the market dimension scores 2.

**The round leaves little new money.** The USD 1.7M committed is already inside the USD 1.05M of cash, so only USD 1.3M of new money remains, USD 0.8M after our ticket. At USD 150K a month of net burn, which will likely rise with the four-state expansion (INFERRED), the full round adds roughly nine months of runway. The co-investor is a Mexico City seed fund whose name is PENDING, plus angels.

### S6 Impact

- **Score:** 3/5
- **Rating:** MODERATE
- **Gate:** passed (the gate disqualifies only a score of 1).
- **Key finding:** Revenue and impact run on the same engine: each new shop taking cards is a shop gaining digital acceptance. But the impact figures are deck claims with unknown method, and the bank-account requirement and new competitors weaken who benefits and what Monedari adds.
- **Evidence:** Deck slides 5 and 12; fund-thesis impact lens; market analysis (bank-account gate, 63.0% account ownership in Mexico); competition analysis (2026 tap-to-phone launches, incumbents' onboarding friction).
- **Gap:** The impact template (not yet produced; it follows this scorecard in the process); the source data behind the 61%, 58% and 11% figures; a client call with merchants.

**The link to revenue is tight.** Monedari earns its main revenue (1.17% of card volume) only when a neighborhood shop accepts a card. Its merchants are shops banks do not visit (deck slide 2), and 61% had never accepted a digital payment before (deck slide 12). That passes the fund's rule that the action growing revenue is the action growing impact.

**The KPIs are partial.** The 61% first-time acceptance and 58% women-run shares could come from onboarding data, but the deck gives no method. "Sales up 11% after six months" is merchant-reported (deck slide 12). None is yet a quarterly operating KPI.

**Who benefits is narrower than the deck says.** The deck says every new shop is "entering the formal financial system" (slide 12). But onboarding requires a bank account for payouts (slide 5), so the owner is already banked. The 37% of Mexican adults without a formal account cannot be served (market analysis). The real outcome is digital acceptance and a sales record for already-banked shops, which still fits the fund's lens but is a smaller claim.

**Contribution is weakening.** With Clip, Mercado Pago and banks now offering phone-based acceptance, the counterfactual for many shops is another provider, not cash. Monedari's contribution rests on reaching low-digital shops through route reps that self-serve funnels miss (competition analysis, INFERRED). Impact risk includes Adelanto: balance-sheet loans with 7% losses and no credit specialist could harm the shops they aim to help if underwriting slips (INFERRED).

## Pattern across dimensions

**Strong people and numbers, weak ground.** The two heaviest commercial scores, S1 and S4, are strong. The dimension that sets the ceiling, S2, is weak, and S3 shows the product has been copied. This is a company likely to execute well inside a niche that is small and contested. The investment case depends on a second act, revenue per shop multiplying through lending and supplier tools, that is today a 640-advance pilot with losses unverified.

**The ADVANCE is sensitive to two scores.** The table shows how the total moves if diligence confirms the two open concerns most likely to lower a score.
